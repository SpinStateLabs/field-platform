## Conditions
### C1 (MEDIUM, confirmed by running): the same 64-hex value passes `content_problem` (result None) when written with
- groups of 9 or 10 digits (space or colon): the 8-digit group limit; groups of 11 return `opaque_run` only by the older rule, not this one;
- a doubled separator: `": "`, `" - "`, `", "` (comma and comma+space), also `|`, a tab, a newline;
- `0x` or `\x` byte prefixes (`0x01 0x23 ...`), a no-break space or zero-width space as the separator.
Slash returned `opaque_run` through the older rule. The card named single-separator forms; these are the next most common spellings of a fingerprint or a hash (an OpenSSL-style `": "` label, a byte dump, a table cell). Fix, one way: count hex digits across a run of up to 3 non-alphanumeric separator characters of any kind with groups up to 16, with the same 12-digit threshold; tests for each spelling above, and a boundary test at group size 8 versus 9 (kills H10 to H12).

### C2 (LOW): the stated trade-off is real and unpinned
The PR says a separated run of 12 or more digits is withheld even when harmless (a digits-only date and time). No test pins that, so a later tightening could silently re-open it; a benign list (`20261106 123045`) asserted as withheld would document the choice.
