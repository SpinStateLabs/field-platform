## Objections first (FORCE-O)
1. The PR chooses to refuse the WHOLE halt when ONE registry row is malformed. A fleet halt with no explicit `domains` asks the registry for every agent; if the answer is a dict, a string, null, a row that is not an object, or a row whose `domain` is not a string, the console answers 502 with fixed text ("pass domains explicitly") and sends no kill (asserted: the kill stub's call list is empty). The reasoning in the docstring is sound ("skipping a row would leave an agent running in an emergency"), but the cost is that a single bad row also stops every well-formed domain from being halted; the operator has to know the domains and retype them under pressure. The alternative (halt the good domains and report the bad row) trades a partial halt for a clear signal; neither is wrong, the choice is Don's. MEDIUM as a design note; the failure mode is loud (502), not silent.
2. Retired rows are skipped before their domain is looked at (pinned: a retired row with `domain: 5` is ignored, H5 killed); an agent whose status is anything other than `retired` (`killed`, `revoked`, a typo) still has its domain enumerated and halted. That is the existing rule (`status != "retired"`), unchanged.
3. The 502 text is fixed and the registry's text is never echoed (LEAK marker absent for all nine shapes, H13 killed). The existing unavailable-registry 502 still carries `_error_text(status, agents)` (an upstream status/body fragment, pre-existing, not touched).
4. Domains are de-duplicated and sorted, and an empty or missing domain becomes `general`; both are pinned (H6, H7, H10, H11 killed).
Steelman: before the change `a.get("domain")` on a non-object raised AttributeError, and an unhashable or non-string domain raised TypeError inside the set comprehension, mid-halt, as a 500 with no kill sent and no explanation; and a registry answer that was a dict iterated its keys. Now each is the same fixed 502, no kill call is made, and the retired, `general`-fallback and all-ok logic of a good registry is unchanged (a test halts finance, general and ops once each, in order, and skips the retired domain).
