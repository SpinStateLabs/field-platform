## OBJECTIONS FIRST
1. Three operator spellings of the settled variables are unpinned: the no-colon forms `${HOME=/tmp}`, `${HOME+/x}`, `${HOME?}` / `${HOME?err}`. The code handles them (probe: all four forbidden), but the mutants that drop `=`, `+` or `?` from the settled-variable operator set (`":-=+?}"`) survive the whole file (3 of 3): dropping `=` or `+` lets the later default expansion turn `${HOME=/tmp}/.ssh` into `/tmp/.ssh` (allowed), and dropping `?` leaves `${HOME?}/.ssh` unexpanded (allowed). The card listed `${HOME-/tmp}` and `${HOME:?}`; the colon forms are pinned, the no-colon `=`, `+` and `?` are not. F-C441-1 LOW (add the four spellings above).
2. The parent-hop after an unknown variable is still not refused: `${PWD}/..`, `${PWD}/../..` and `$PWD/../..` are allowed (so is `${OLDPWD}`). This is the declared limit of the checker (an unresolved `${VAR}` is "unknown", C-371 F-C371-2), but `..` after any unresolved variable climbs out of whatever the variable names, so it is a case a static check can refuse without knowing the value. F-C441-2 LOW to MEDIUM (refuse a source that contains `..` after an unresolved variable, or say in the docstring that it is out of scope).
3. Other places one step further out that are allowed: `/Users/<name>/.ssh` and `/Users/${USER}/.aws` (macOS home; the estates are Linux, so LOW), `${XDG_DATA_HOME}/keyrings` (the spelling with a `$HOME` default, `${XDG_DATA_HOME:-$HOME/.local/share}/keyrings`, is refused because `$HOME` is read first; the bare one is allowed because `XDG_DATA_HOME` is not always set and is not in `SETTLED_VARIABLES`). F-C441-3 LOW.
