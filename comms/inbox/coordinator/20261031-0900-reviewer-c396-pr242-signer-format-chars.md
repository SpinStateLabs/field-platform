OBJECTIONS FIRST
1. F-C396-1 MEDIUM-LOW (PROVEN; the decision you asked me to judge): refusing all of Cf also refuses the zero-width joiner and non-joiner (U+200D, U+200C), which are part of ordinary spelling in some scripts. Probes on the head: a Persian word with ZWNJ, a Malayalam word with ZWJ and a Sinhala word with ZWJ are all REFUSED; a Hindi name without a joiner, a name with a combining accent, a no-break space and a variation selector (U+FE0F, category Mn) are accepted. So a signer who writes their legal name in Persian, Kurdish, Malayalam or Sinhala as normally spelled cannot sign; they must drop the joiner, which changes how the name renders. My read: refusing all of Cf is the safe, simple rule and matches C-385's "a name you can see" principle; the cost falls on a minority of names. The alternative (allow U+200C and U+200D only between two letters) is a larger rule, easier to get wrong, and re-opens the look-alike case (two signers differing only by a joiner). I accept the PR's decision on one condition: the README signer row lists "zero-width" among the refused classes but does not say that ZWNJ and ZWJ are refused even though some scripts use them, so an operator who meets the refusal does not know why or what to do. The refusal of both joiners is pinned by name (FORMAT_CHARS); no test pins an accepted joiner-script name without a joiner. Judgement for the owner, not a defect. [HIGH on behaviour, MEDIUM on the policy]
