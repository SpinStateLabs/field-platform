## Findings
1. MEDIUM, PROVEN (probe, ~/ff-work/probe57, FIELD_OPERATOR_REQUIRE=1): the "never silent" claim fails on one branch. Under ENFORCE, `OperatorHealth.note_refused` returns without writing OR counting when no ledger is wired or the ledger getter raises: operator_health.py:340 `if ledger is None: return` (and the `except Exception: ledger = None` above it). Probe: no ledger_getter -> refused_count=1 dropped_refused_records=0; getter raises -> refused_count=1 dropped_refused_records=0; a ledger that answers 403 -> dropped_refused_records=1 (the counted case). Mutant L340 `if ledger is None` -> False survives: untested. Fix: `self.dropped_records += 1` before that return, and test both cases. A refusal count that exceeds written + dropped is exactly the blind spot #52 set out to close.
2. LOW (design): the new counters are per-process and are not witnessed. The C-6 observer reads mode, registry and (R2) generation and break-glass counters from /health; `dropped_unauthenticated_records` and `dropped_refused_records` are only visible if someone reads /health or runs phase_g_check. "Counted" is not "alerted": a nonzero value raises no ledger event and no ntfy. Add them to the observer (event on change) or to the stage controller's abort conditions (P36).
3. LOW (design, ledger side, api.py ~367-371 docstring): a STOP-type event (delegation.revoke, kill.*, lifecycle.decommissioned) whose caller claim does not verify is stored with `caller_unsigned: true` and a 201, never refused. That is intended (a kill must not be blocked by a key fault) but it means a signing-form regression of the kind #52 fixed lands on kill/revoke rows silently as unsigned rows. Only `verify-callers` shows it. Count `caller_unsigned` rows in the ledger health.
4. LOW: `LedgerClient.append` raises `LedgerUnreachableError(f"ledger append returned {status}: {resp.text}")` with the full response body (clients.py, `LedgerClient.append`). #52 avoids the body for its two counters via `append_failure_status`, but any other caller that logs `str(exc)` (the operator layer's other ledger writers, ops-console, the kill-switch) can log the ledger's body. Not a secret by design (403 text is fixed) but not bounded either; clip it.
5. LOW (test gap, from #46 not #52): clients.py:257 `if clash:` in `_with_extra_headers` (header-collision refusal) survives; no test passes a colliding per-call header.
6. INFO: `CallerSigner.from_env` turns any unusable key into an unsigned client with ONE warning per process (clients.py ~100-140). On a signature-required ledger every append is then refused 403 and counted (operator records) or reported by the caller; consistent with fail-closed at the ledger, but the warning is the only trace of why. Include the caller-signer state (`signing: on/off`) in /health.
