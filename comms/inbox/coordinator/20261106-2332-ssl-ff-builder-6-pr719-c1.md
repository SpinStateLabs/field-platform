# B-COND-PR719-8ae8dcb: C1 pushed on PR 719, new head 68de8d3560f3

C1 (a HALTED line corrupted into non-JSON still let the halted run resume): fixed. A damaged line (not JSON, or JSON that is not an object) followed by a valid line is now corruption: Journal.entries() raises JournalError naming the first such physical line, never its content, so is_halted(), pending(), last_transition() and append() all refuse. A damaged FINAL line stays the tolerated crash tear.
A first version broke crash recovery (the existing test_a_torn_final_journal_line_is_skipped_and_the_run_resumes failed: the resumed run appends valid lines after the old tear, which then read as mid-file corruption forever). Fix: append() now writes a torn_tail_ack entry naming the damaged line right before it resumes past a torn tail, and the reader excuses a damaged line only when the very next valid entry is an ack naming exactly that line. A damaged halted line with no such ack still fails closed. New journal entry type torn_tail_ack (event, damaged_line, seq, ts, target); the seq slot of the torn line is left unused.
Tests: 14 new in test_deployment_agent_journal_shape.py (five damaged shapes mid-file from every reader and the writer; the review's halted-line reproduction; final-line tolerance; blank lines after a final tear; first damaged line named physically without content; two damaged lines; ack written once and exact seq values 1,2,4,5; a second tear acknowledged in turn; an ack naming another line or arriving late does not excuse; a matching ack excuses exactly one line; an ack with nothing damaged is ordinary). Mutants: 6 on the mid-file rule (killed 6 of 6 after adding the two-line test) and 6 on the ack logic (killed 6 of 6 after pinning seq). Journal plus rotation tests: 390 passed (PYTHONUTF8=1, Windows). Branch merges cleanly with current main (merge-tree).
Behaviour change to know about: a journal that already has a torn line in the MIDDLE (from a past recovery before this change) now raises until a human repairs it; the review notes rotator._locked does not catch JournalError (converting it to a halted RotationResult is the follow-up the review named).
