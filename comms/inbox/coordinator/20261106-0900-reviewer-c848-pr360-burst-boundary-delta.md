OBJECTIONS FIRST
1. F-C848-1 MEDIUM (PROVEN, predates this PR, outside its diff): a clock step BACKWARDS makes the meter UNDER-count once the clock is corrected. Rows are stamped with the server's wall clock (api.py:504-509 usage, record_spend path for /spend) and every window is `ts >= start` with NO upper bound (core.py:780-787, :613-617). Probe: daily cap, 300 cents at D+1 00:00:05, clock steps back 10 s, 400 cents stamped D 23:59:55, clock corrected: GET /status on D+1 reports 300, not 700 (the 400 spent in real day D+1 is billed to day D). Rolling burst: limit 300 tokens per 60 s, clock 70 s behind for two 100-token events, then corrected: the third event (300 real tokens inside 60 real seconds) is NOT flagged. While the clock is stepped back the meter OVER-counts (future-stamped rows still count: 700 seen on day D; a burst is flagged early), which is the safe direction. What a wrong answer lets a caller do: this is a meter, not a gate, so the effect is that ESCALATE/BLOCK in /status and the rogue_burst escalation arrive late or not at all for spend made during the step; at a step across midnight (or the first of the month) up to one full cap of extra spend can be reported as OK. The caller cannot cause the step; an operator or NTP does. [HIGH, run]
2. F-C848-2 LOW (PROVEN): the new docstring (core.py:397-405) says a stored row with an unknown period makes "GET /caps and POST /usage" raise. Probe (period set to weekly, Daily and empty by SQL): SIX routes answer 500: GET /caps, POST /spend, POST /usage, GET /status, GET /totals, GET /usage; and neither the /spend nor the /usage event is recorded (spend rows stay 1, usage rows stay 0). So on such a row the meter does not "count more", it stops counting and the caller gets a bare 500. The docstring correctly leaves the policy open; it understates the reach. C-853 objection 2 found five routes; GET /usage is the sixth. [HIGH, run]
